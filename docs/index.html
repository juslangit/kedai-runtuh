Kedai Runtuh

Kedai Runtuh is a one-thumb casual mobile game about stacking a mamak order as high as possible before it collapses. Food hangs from a swinging hook above the tower; one tap drops it. Land it on top and the tower grows, the camera rises and the swing speeds up. Miss, or knock the stack over, and the run ends. It is aimed at the same audience that shares short mobile-game clips — the appeal is that a collapse is funny and never looks the same twice, so the game generates its own video content.

Why this project exists

It is Luqman's first game as a sellable/publishable product rather than coursework, and it is deliberately scoped to play to his strengths. The work is roughly 80% 3D art and 20% code: the engine supplies the physics, so the game design reduces to choosing shapes, sizes and a handful of tuning numbers. It also doubles as teaching material at Todak Academy, since the whole game is small enough to read end to end.

How it plays

  1. A piece of food hangs from a hook that swings left and right above the tower.
  2. One tap releases it. It falls with real physics — no snapping, no grid.
  3. If it settles near the top of the tower, the score goes up, the camera rises and the hook swings faster.
  4. If it slides down the side, lands on the table, or the tower falls over, the run ends and the score is shown against the player's best.

01-idea.md · 6 sections

Idea

In one sentence

Stack a mamak order — plates, bowls, glasses of teh, roti canai — as high as you can with one tap, and watch it collapse.

Where it came from

On 2026-09-07 Luqman asked for a casual game idea that could go viral. Three were put forward: a delivery-rider physics game, this stacking game, and a 3D-printer drawing game. He picked the stacker because it is the smallest of the three that still has a real hook, and therefore the one most likely to actually get finished.

The hook

The test applied to every idea was: can a stranger understand the whole game from three seconds of muted video? A tower of food wobbling and falling passes that test with no UI, no text and no explanation. This matters more than any other design consideration, because it is the thing that decides whether a clip spreads.

Two properties do the work:

  • Failure is funnier than success. Nobody shares a clean stack. Everybody shares the moment eleven plates and a glass of teh go over the edge.
  • Physics never repeats. Every collapse is different, so there is an endless supply of new clips, and the players make them, not Luqman.

Why this one and not the others

The delivery-rider game reads even better on video but needs a vehicle, a road, a traffic system and a camera that follows movement. The stacker needs a table, a hook and some food. Same hook, a fraction of the work — and the local flavour (mamak food, Malay wording like runtuh and tersasar) is free novelty for an audience that has not seen itself in a game.

What makes it Malaysian rather than generic

The stacking genre is old. What is not old is stacking this — pinggan, mangkuk, gelas teh tarik, roti canai, kotak bungkus — on a mamak table, with Malay words on the game-over screen. That is the difference between "another stacking game" and one that a Malaysian audience wants to send to a friend.

Not in scope for v1

Multiplayer, characters, a story, levels, in-app purchases, ads. None of these make the three-second clip better, and each one is a reason the game never ships.

02-planning.md · 4 sections

Planning

Definition of done for v1

An Android build, in portrait, where a player can tap to drop food, build a tower, fail, see their score against their best, and immediately try again. Nothing else.

Phases

Phase 1 — grey-box prototype (in progress, started 2026-09-07)

Primitive shapes only, no art. The whole point of this phase is to answer one question: is dropping and stacking this actually fun? If it is not fun with grey boxes, no amount of Sketchfab art will save it, and the project should be stopped here rather than decorated.

  • done Godot project, portrait, one scene
  • done Swinging hook, one-tap drop
  • done Physics stacking with the camera rising as the tower grows
  • done Score, best score saved between sessions, restart
  • done Two distinct failure states — runtuh (collapse) and tersasar (missed)
  • to do Tuning so a competent player can reach a double-digit score
  • to do Luqman plays it and says whether it is fun

Phase 2 — art pass

Replace primitives with real models. Sketchfab is the source; each item becomes a .glb in assets/models/ and a path in the ITEMS list in scripts/game.gd.

  • Check the licence on every model before it goes in the repo
  • Keep the collision shapes as simple primitives even when the visible mesh is detailed — accurate collision on a detailed mesh is slow and stacks badly
  • Kedai background: table, stools, a wall, warm evening light

Phase 3 — feel and polish

Sound on landing and collapse, a small camera shake, a wobble warning before the tower goes, slow-motion on the collapse (this is the part that makes the clip).

Phase 4 — ship

Android export, icon, store listing, a handful of recorded clips.

Deliberately deferred

Leaderboards, daily challenges, unlockable food, an endless-vs-levels mode. All of these are reasonable and none of them belong before the game is proven fun.

Main risk

The genre is crowded and the game has to feel good, not just work. That is why Phase 1 ends with a play test and an honest go/no-go rather than with a feature.

Decisions

D-001 — Godot 4 instead of Unreal Engine 5
  • Date: 2026-09-07
  • Status: active
  • Context: Luqman's usual game engine is Unreal Engine 5, but this is a small 2.5D mobile game that has to export to Android and stay light.
  • Options considered:
OptionProsCons
Godot 4Tiny Android builds, fast iteration, GDScript is readable, already installed with an MCP bridge in the terminalNew engine to learn
Unreal Engine 5Already knows it, better rendererHeavy for a phone game, slow iteration, huge builds
  • Decision: Godot 4.7.2. Luqman asked for it directly.
  • Why: For a game this small the Unreal renderer buys nothing, and the build size and iteration speed matter a lot on mobile.
D-002 — Sketchfab as the art source
  • Date: 2026-09-07
  • Status: active
  • Context: The game needs a dozen food models. Modelling them all first would delay finding out whether the game is fun.
  • Decision: Sketchfab, downloaded as .glb into assets/models/.
  • Why: It gets art in fast, and Luqman can replace any model with his own Blender work later without touching code — each item is one line in a list.
  • Watch out: Licences vary per model. Check each one before it goes in the repo, and keep a note of the source in 07-references.md.
D-003 — Fixed side camera, not a free orbit camera
  • Date: 2026-09-07
  • Status: active
  • Context: The game is 3D, so the camera could be free.
  • Decision: The camera is locked side-on and only rises as the tower grows.
  • Why: The tower's silhouette has to be readable at a glance in a muted video clip, and depth is hard to judge on a phone. With a free camera, players blame the camera for their misses instead of themselves.
D-004 — Physics locked to a flat plane
  • Date: 2026-09-07
  • Status: active
  • Context: Full 3D stacking physics is chaotic and unreadable from a fixed side view — pieces drift toward and away from the camera where the player cannot see it.
  • Decision: Every dropped piece sets axis_lock_linear_z, axis_lock_angular_x and axis_lock_angular_y. Pieces move only left/right and up/down, and rotate only in the plane of the screen.
  • Why: The game is 3D to look at and 2D to play. Everything that affects the outcome is visible on screen, so a loss always looks fair.
D-005 — Swinging hook with a one-tap drop
  • Date: 2026-09-07
  • Status: active
  • Options considered:
OptionProsCons
Swinging hook, one tapOne thumb, no tutorial, the swing speed is the difficulty curveLess control
Drag and place with a fingerFull controlSlow, no time pressure, a collapse feels like the player's fault rather than the game's joke
Conveyor belt, tap to grabAdds item choiceTwo decisions per turn, needs a tutorial
  • Decision: Swinging hook.
  • Why: It needs no explanation, and the swing speeding up with the score gives a difficulty curve for free — one number, no level design.
D-006 — Freeze the lower tower, keep the top few pieces live
  • Date: 2026-09-07
  • Status: active
  • Context: With every piece left physically active, the whole tower slowly crept and drifted, and pieces slid off a stack that had been hit accurately. Automated play testing capped out at 2–4 pieces, which is not a game.
  • Options considered:
OptionProsCons
Freeze every landed pieceRock solidNothing can ever collapse — kills the premise and the name
Leave everything liveBest collapsesUnplayable; the tower will not stand up
Freeze all but the top fewStable base, the top still collapsesThe collapse is partial rather than total
  • Decision: Freeze all but the newest LIVE_PIECES (currently 3) pieces.
  • Why: The collapse is the product, so it cannot be removed — but only the top of a tower realistically goes over anyway. This keeps the drama where the player is looking and stops the base wandering off the table.
D-007 — Two named failure states instead of one
  • Date: 2026-09-07
  • Status: active
  • Decision: RUNTUH! when a piece falls off the table (a collapse), and TERSASAR! when a piece comes to rest more than LANDING_TOLERANCE below the top of the tower (a miss).
  • Why: A player who does not know why they lost blames the game. Naming the two failures separately makes every loss legible, and the Malay wording is part of what makes the game feel local rather than generic.
D-008 — Private GitHub repo for now
  • Date: 2026-09-07
  • Status: active
  • Decision: juslangit/kedai-runtuh, private.
  • Why: Keeps the option of going public later without exposing early mistakes or committing to asset licences before they have been checked.
D-009 — One asset pack, not one model per item
  • Date: 2026-09-07
  • Status: active
  • Context: Phase 2 needed real models for six items. Searching Sketchfab per item returned good individual models, but from six different authors in six different styles — a museum-scanned bowl next to a PSX-era cardboard box.
  • Decision: Take everything from a single pack — Stylized Food & Cafe Props Pack by Pollypipe, CC-BY-4.0.
  • Why: A stacking game shows every item next to every other item at all times, so a mixed art style is more obvious here than in almost any other genre. One author means one style for free.
  • Trade-off: The pack has no roti canai, so Roti canai became Telur mata (a fried egg) and a donut became Kuih keria, which is a real Malay sweet potato doughnut. The food had to bend to the pack rather than the other way round.
D-010 — Models are fitted to the physics box, never the reverse
  • Date: 2026-09-07
  • Status: active
  • Context: Luqman approved the mechanics before the art went in, so the art pass must not change how the game plays.
  • Decision: width and height in the ITEMS list define the collision box. At startup each model is measured and scaled — non-uniformly if it has to be — so it exactly fills that box. Collision shapes stay simple cylinders and boxes.
  • Why: Swapping a model can then never alter the tuning. Automated play scored the same before and after the art went in, which confirmed it. It also means the visible mesh and the physics agree exactly, so a loss never looks unfair.
  • Cost: Very flat models get stretched vertically — the plate is about 2.4x thicker than authored. In a stylised game this is invisible, and arguably reads better at phone size. If a model ever looks wrong, its height is the number to change, not the model.
D-011 — The kitchen is a real room the camera sits outside of
  • Date: 2026-09-07
  • Status: active
  • Context: Luqman asked for a kitchen background. A flat painted backdrop was the easy option; a real 3D room was the better one.
  • Decision: A full low-poly kitchen room (Mumladze28, CC-BY-4.0) placed in world space behind the table, scaled 6x, turned 180 degrees so the counter run faces the camera. The camera sits outside the near wall, which culls away because the room's walls face inward — so you look straight into the kitchen.
  • Why: It gives real depth and parallax, and because it is static rather than parented to the camera, it scrolls away below you as the tower climbs. That is what sells the sense of height. The gradient backdrop stays behind it and takes over once you climb past the ceiling.
  • Known trade-off: At this scale the food is roughly three times too big for the kitchen — our cawan is about the size of the microwave. It reads as a deliberate stylisation, but the Kitchen node's scale is the one number to change if Luqman wants it corrected.
D-012 — The rope is simulated, but the game does not read it
  • Date: 2026-09-07
  • Status: active
  • Context: Luqman asked for the rope to hang from a fixed pivot at top centre and to behave like a rope rather than a rigid bar. The risk was that adding physics to the rope would change where pieces are released and undo the tuning he had already approved.
  • Decision: A verlet rope of 16 points, pinned at both ends — top to a fixed pivot above the middle of the table, bottom to the hook. The hook still moves on exactly the same sin path as before. The rope is decoration only; nothing in the game reads its position.
  • Why: It looks like a real rope — it bends, trails behind the swing and settles — while being provably unable to affect play. Automated scores were unchanged after it went in.
  • Tuning note: The first version had too much slack and momentum, so the lower half whipped past the hanging piece and looked like the rope was skewering the food. Less slack, heavier damping and more solver passes fixed it. The rope also now ends at the top of the hanging item rather than its centre, so it reads as tied on.
D-013 — The hook is a pendulum, not a slider
  • Date: 2026-09-07
  • Status: active
  • Context: Luqman: "make the object swing realistically, not go left to right like that." The hook had been setting position.x = sin(t) * range at a fixed height, so the food slid along a flat horizontal line.
  • Decision: The hook now swings on an arc around the pivot: position = pivot + Vector3(sin(angle), -cos(angle), 0) * length, with angle = swing_degrees * sin(t). The food also leans with the rope, and keeps that lean when released (DROP_TILT).
  • Why the angle is driven and not simulated: a real simulated pendulum loses energy and would need pushing to keep swinging, and the swing speed is the game's entire difficulty curve — it has to be exact. Driving the angle gives a true arc with completely predictable timing.
  • Effect on play: the food is about 0.45 higher at the ends of the swing than at the bottom. Dropping at the ends is therefore slightly harder, which is honest — you are dropping from higher up.
  • DROP_TILT is a deliberate knob. At 1.0 what you see hanging is what you drop, which is honest but adds variance (automated runs 10, 10, 10, 3, 4, 10). At 0.0 pieces always release upright and results are steadier (11, 11, 9, 10, 8, 8). Left at 1.0 because visual and physics agreeing matters more, but it is one number to change.
D-014 — Settle detection was the real reason the game felt slow
  • Date: 2026-09-07
  • Status: active
  • Context: After the pendulum went in, automated scores dropped from 4-10 to 3-6 and nearly every run hit the test's frame cap. The pendulum looked like the culprit. It was not — removing the new tilt made things worse, which is what proved the theory wrong and forced an actual measurement.
  • What was happening: printing drop_timer at every landing showed the first four pieces settling in about a second, and everything after that timing out at the full five seconds with velocities of 0.31 to 0.40 — just above the 0.30 "stopped moving" threshold. A tower two pieces deep in live physics never goes completely still, so past four pieces every drop waited out the timeout. The game paused for five seconds after each drop.
  • Decision: SETTLE_SPEED 0.30 to 0.55, SETTLE_TIME 0.35 to 0.30, MAX_DROP_TIME 5.0 to 2.5.
  • Result: automated scores went from 2-10 (median 5) to 8-11 (median 9.5).
  • Why it matters beyond this fix: MAX_DROP_TIME was written as a safety net and had quietly become the normal path. A fallback that fires routinely is a bug report, not a fallback — worth checking whenever something feels sluggish.
D-015 — The high score lives outside every scene
  • Date: 2026-09-07
  • Status: active
  • Context: Luqman: "the high score will be saved elsewhere so everytime a player play the game, the highscore will not changed." The score had been read and written inside the game scene itself.
  • Decision: scripts/save_data.gd is registered as an autoload called SaveData — one object that exists outside the scene tree and survives scene changes. It owns the high score and the sound setting, and writes both to user://kedai_runtuh.cfg the moment either changes.
  • Why this is the right shape and not just tidier: restarting builds a completely fresh game scene whose score starts at zero. Because the high score was never part of that scene, there is nothing for a restart to reset — the guarantee comes from where the data lives, not from remembering to preserve it. It also means the main menu can show the best score without loading the game.
  • Verified: _persist.tscn submits 17, then submits 5 and confirms the record stays 17, reloads from disk, then builds a real game scene and checks it reads score 0 with best 17.
D-016 — One theme file, no per-scene colours
  • Date: 2026-09-07
  • Status: active
  • Context: A main menu, a HUD, a pause menu and a game-over panel is enough UI that styling it node by node would guarantee drift.
  • Decision: scripts/ui_theme.gd builds a single Theme in code. No scene sets its own colours. Buttons choose between three looks with Godot's theme_type_variation: PrimaryButton (red, the one you are meant to press), IconButton (small, for pause), and the default cream one.
  • Why built in code rather than a .tres resource: the palette is then eight named constants at the top of one readable file, which is a better place for Luqman to change a colour than a tree of theme overrides in the inspector.
  • Caught by looking at it: the first version used the same cream for buttons and for the panel behind them, so the secondary buttons vanished into the panel and read as plain text. Buttons are now a deeper tan than the panel.
D-017 — Pause by pausing the tree, with the UI exempt
  • Date: 2026-09-07
  • Status: active
  • Decision: get_tree().paused = true. The game root and the UI layer are set to PROCESS_MODE_ALWAYS; _process returns immediately when paused, and taps are ignored while paused so they cannot drop a piece behind the menu.
  • Why the game root is ALWAYS rather than pausable: so it can still receive the Escape key and the Android back button to unpause. A node that pauses itself cannot hear the input that would resume it.
  • Everything else — the swing, the rope, the falling piece — is left pausable, so it all stops dead and resumes exactly where it was.
D-018 — All interface text in English; the title stays Malay
  • Date: 2026-09-07
  • Status: active
  • Context: The UI had been written entirely in Malay — SKOR, TERBAIK, BERHENTI, SAMBUNG, RUNTUH!, TERSASAR! — on the reasoning that a Malaysian-themed game deserved Malay wording. Luqman asked to "change the overall game language into english language".
  • Decision: Every menu, button, HUD label and message is English. The internal food names in ITEMS were translated too (Pinggan to Plate, Kuih keria to Doughnut), because the project convention is that code is English.
  • What was kept: the game is still called Kedai Runtuh. It is a proper noun — the title, the repo name, the project folder and this knowledge-base entry all carry it, and a name is not interface copy.
  • Lesson worth remembering: a Malaysian subject does not imply a Malay interface. English is the default for anything a player reads, and the theme lives in the food and the setting, not in the button labels.
  • What did NOT change: the food itself. The game is still a mamak order — the models, the kitchen and the premise are untouched.
D-019 — The UI can take bought artwork, but the artwork is not in the repo
  • Date: 2026-09-07
  • Status: blocked on a purchase
  • Context: Luqman asked to restyle the UI with the Cozy UI Pack (https://dobo-ui.itch.io/cozy-ui).
  • Finding: the pack is name-your-own-price with a $8.54 minimum — it is not free. Only a 357 kB demo is available without paying, and even that comes through itch.io's own download flow rather than a direct link. The assets could not be obtained, and buying them is Luqman's decision to make, not something to do on his behalf.
  • Decision: build the swap mechanism now and leave the artwork slot empty. A SPRITES block at the top of scripts/ui_theme.gd maps four roles — ordinary button, its pressed state, primary button, panel — to file paths. A filled-in path becomes a 9-sliced StyleBoxTexture; an empty one keeps the drawn box, so the game always runs and pieces can be swapped one at a time. Padding is copied from the drawn style being replaced, so the layout does not shift.
  • Licence note to check before shipping: the pack allows commercial use and does not require attribution, but it forbids redistributing the assets and forbids "use in AI/NFT projects". The redistribution clause matters here — if the repo is ever made public, the pack's PNGs should not be committed to it.
D-020 — All audio is CC0, chosen for that reason
  • Date: 2026-09-07
  • Status: active
  • Sources: Kenney's Interface Sounds and Impact Sounds, and the track Ramen from HoliznaCC0's album Lo-fi And Chill (Free Music Archive). All CC0 1.0.

D-026 — GL Compatibility is the Android renderer, and the APK carries x86_64
  • Date: 2026-09-07
  • Status: active
  • What went wrong: the first APK was built arm64 only, because the export preset was filled in without asking where it would be tested. Luqman tried it in BlueStacks on a PC, which is x86_64, and got a black screen. Separately, the Vulkan build never renders in any emulated environment.
  • Decisions:
    1. architectures/x86_64=true alongside arm64-v8a. The APK goes from 36 MB to 63 MB, which is the cost of running in an emulator at all.
    2. renderer/rendering_method.mobile="gl_compatibility". Vulkan initialises in the Android emulator and reports the real Apple M2 GPU, then fails every present with VkResult 5 and sits at 0% CPU. GL Compatibility runs at 10-20% CPU and plays properly. It also runs on far more phones. The cost is the subtle glow.
    3. anti_aliasing/quality/msaa_3d.mobile=0. 4x MSAA is expensive on a phone and is among the first things an emulated GPU gets wrong.

D-031 — Settled pieces are put to sleep, which is what stops the buzzing
  • Date: 2026-09-07
  • Status: active

  • What was happening: two rigid bodies resting on each other never quite stop. Godot only sleeps a body once it falls below sleep_threshold_linear (default 0.1) and _angular (default 0.14). The stack idles at roughly 0.116 linear and 0.223 angular — just above both. So pieces never slept and ground against each other forever.
  • Fix, in three parts:
    1. _settle_tower() puts every settled piece to sleep. Contact wakes them again, so the game plays identically.
    2. Sleep thresholds raised in project.godot — 0.25 linear, 0.5 angular, 0.3s before sleeping.
    3. The righting torque now only ever touches the piece currently falling, and has a damping term, so it can never disturb a settled tower.
  • Measured on an untouched tower over four seconds: worst angular velocity went from 0.413 (averaged over four towers) to 0.000, and pieces still moving from 1.0 to 0.
  • Checked it did not break the game: 4 of 4 pieces asleep, then a landing wakes the stack and it reacts at 5.14 velocity. Scores and edge-standing unchanged.
D-032 — Two measurement mistakes worth not repeating
  • Date: 2026-09-07
  • Status: active

Measuring the wrong property. D-029 claimed the righting torque cut edge-standing from 24% to 4%. It measured each piece's resting angle — but never whether the piece was actually at rest. Some of that "success" was pieces buzzing around upright rather than settling upright. The number was real and the conclusion was wrong. When measuring whether something has settled, measure velocity, not position.

Measuring at the wrong moment. The first jitter test reported angular velocities of 5 to 7 and looked catastrophic. It was reading a piece still falling, and sometimes a run that had ended mid-measurement and unfrozen the whole tower for the collapse. Fixed by waiting for the game to return to AIMING and aborting if the run ends. Only then did the real numbers appear, and they were small.

Both cost a full round of wrong conclusions. Before trusting a measurement, ask what state the thing is in when the number is taken.

04-methods.md · 11 sections

Methods

Automated play testing in headless Godot

The single most useful technique found on day one. Godot runs a scene with no window at all:

/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot --headless --path . res://tools/dev/checks/autoplay.tscn

A throwaway scene loads main.tscn, calls the game's own _drop() on a timer while aiming at the top of the tower, and prints the score when the run ends. Running it six times gives a distribution of scores in about a minute.

Why this matters: it turned "the game feels hard" into "a competent player scores 2–4", which is a number that can be tuned against and re-measured. It also caught two bugs that would have been slow to find by hand — an infinite hang when two pieces jittered against each other forever, and a scoring rule that credited pieces which had slid down the side of the pile.

It is not a substitute for playing it. The bot measures whether the game works; only a person can say whether it is fun.

Tuning constants live at the top of the file

Everything that decides how the game feels — gravity, drop height, swing speed, how still a piece must be before it counts, how far below the top still counts as landed — is a named constant at the top of scripts/game.gd, with a comment. No tuning number is buried in the middle of a function. Changing how the game feels should never require reading the game loop.

Adding food is a one-line change

scripts/game.gd opens with an ITEMS list. Each entry is a name, a shape (box or cylinder), a size, a colour and a model path. Adding a new item to the game is adding a line to that list. When a Sketchfab model replaces a primitive, only the model field changes.

Keep collision shapes primitive

Even after real models go in, the collision shape stays a box or a cylinder. A detailed mesh collider is slow on a phone and stacks badly — small surface details make pieces perch and jitter instead of settling. The player sees the nice model; the physics sees a box.

Measuring a piece's real extent

_world_box() returns the piece's true world-space AABB by multiplying the mesh instance's global transform by its local AABB. This is what the landing rule and the camera height are based on, so a piece that has tipped over is measured by where it actually is rather than by the size it was spawned with.

Tower height is recomputed, never accumulated

_tower_top() measures the tallest settled piece from scratch every time a piece lands. An earlier version kept a running maximum, which meant that if the stack settled downward the recorded height stayed at the old high-water mark and every subsequent drop was wrongly judged a miss.

Snap the logic, glide the presentation

The hook's height and the camera's height both track the top of the tower, but they must not be treated the same way. The camera glides because that looks good. The hook snaps, because the game spawns the next piece at the hook and therefore needs it to be where the logic thinks it is. While both glided, a fast tap spawned a piece from below its intended height — occasionally inside the tower — and the stack exploded for no visible reason.

The general rule: anything the game logic reads a position from should snap. Only things that are purely presentational should be animated.

Final Phase 1 tuning

Recorded so they can be recovered if a later change makes things worse: gravity 11.0, DROP_HEIGHT 1.4, LIVE_PIECES 2, LANDING_TOLERANCE 0.55, SETTLE_TIME 0.35, friction 2.0, linear_damp 1.0, angular_damp 3.0, hook swing_range 1.8, base_speed 1.4 rising by 0.055 per point.

Automated play at these numbers scores 2–10, median about 5, with some runs still standing when the test's frame cap ends them.

project.godot takes ; comments, not #

Godot's project.godot is a config file whose comment character is ;. A # comment does not error — it silently swallows the setting on the next line.

This cost an hour. A # comment was added above textures/vram_compression/import_etc2_astc=true, which removed that setting. The Vulkan mobile renderer requires ETC2/ASTC compression, so the Android export began failing with the message "Cannot export project with preset Android due to configuration errors:" — and then nothing, no reason given.

Worse, it looked like the renderer change had caused it, because the export had worked moments earlier under GL Compatibility (which does not require ETC2). The real cause was a comment, not a renderer.

If an export breaks for no visible reason, check project.godot for #.

The Android emulator: Vulkan never presents, GL Compatibility works

See D-026. Vulkan initialises but never presents a frame; GL Compatibility runs. adb screencap returns black for GPU surfaces even when drawing — use CPU load from adb shell top to tell whether a game is actually rendering.

The project record: docs/index.html (2026-09-15)

One HTML file holds the whole record of the game — overview, pipeline, captioned screenshots, idea, planning, milestones, decisions, methods, session logs, test files and git history — in the repo and at a private link. Luqman's standing rule: whenever the game changes, rebuild it and republish, the same as Referee For Fun.

then publish that copy to https://claude.ai/artifact/DEHnMjnwaX4awbdwZCyLFF (pass it as url, so the link stays the same). These notes are read directly, so writing the knowledge base is most of updating the record. New screenshots need an entry in GALLERIES at the top of the script; a missing file shows as a labelled gap, not an error. Everything below the script's "engine" line is identical in all four game copies (auxetic-chess, bluff-show, kedai-runtuh, scribble-relay) — change it in all of them at once.

Luqman tried the APK in BlueStacks on a PC and got a black screen. That turned out to be the useful failure, because it exposed three separate bugs.

1. The APK was arm64 only

The export preset was filled in without asking where it would be tested. BlueStacks on a PC is x86_64, so the APK could not run there at all. Added architectures/x86_64=true. The APK goes 36 MB to 63 MB, which is simply what it costs to run in an emulator (D-026).

2. Vulkan never presents a frame in an emulator

An earlier note in this log said the emulator could not run the game. That was wrong, and is corrected. It cannot run the Vulkan build. Vulkan initialises and reports the real GPU — Vulkan 1.3.0 - Forward Mobile - Using Device #0: Apple - Apple M2 — then fails every present with VkResult 5 and sits at 0.0% CPU showing black.

Session 10 — the vibrating items

Luqman: "sometimes the items start to vibrate, i think this have something to do with the collision". He was right.

The cause

Two rigid bodies resting on each other never quite stop. Godot only sleeps a body once it falls below sleep_threshold_linear (default 0.1) and _angular (default 0.14). The stack idles at about 0.116 linear and 0.223 angular — just above both. So nothing ever slept, and the pieces ground against each other forever at a speed too small to read as movement but big enough to look like a buzz (D-031).

The fix

Settled pieces are put to sleep in _settle_tower(). Contact wakes them, so the game plays identically — checked explicitly: 4 of 4 asleep, then a landing wakes the stack and it reacts at 5.14 velocity. Sleep thresholds were raised, and the righting torque now only touches the piece currently falling.

Measured on an untouched tower over four seconds: worst angular velocity 0.413 to 0.000, pieces still moving 1.0 to 0.

Two measurement mistakes, both mine

I measured the wrong property. Last session I claimed the righting torque cut edge-standing from 24% to 4%. That measured each piece's resting angle and never checked whether it was actually at rest — so part of that "success" was pieces buzzing around upright. The number was right and the conclusion was wrong. That torque was contributing to the very bug Luqman reported.

I measured at the wrong moment. The first jitter test reported angular velocities of 5 to 7 and looked alarming. It was reading a piece still in mid-air, and sometimes a run that had ended during the measurement and unfrozen the whole tower for the collapse. Once it waited for a genuinely idle tower, the real numbers appeared and they were small.

Both written up in D-032.

Also worth noting

I assumed at the start that my own new righting torque was the cause, because it was the newest code. Averaged over four towers it made no measurable difference to jitter (0.413 against 0.452). The real cause predated it by a long way.

The game itself. SaveData and Audio are autoloads; the rest hang off the scenes.

FileWhat it does
scripts/audio.gdAll the game's sound in one place, registered as the autoload Audio.
scripts/fit_model.gdScales and positions a decorative model so it lines up with the game.
scripts/game.gdKedai Runtuh — main game loop.
scripts/haptics.gdShort vibrations, in one place so the whole game's feel can be tuned together.
scripts/hook.gdThe hook the food hangs from.
scripts/main_menu.gdThe title screen. The kitchen behind it is the real 3D scene, not a picture, so the menu and the game look like the same place.
scripts/rope.gdA soft rope hanging from a fixed pivot down to the swinging hook.
scripts/save_data.gdEverything that has to outlive a single run: the high score and the sound setting. Registered as an autoload called SaveData, so every scene sees the same copy and nothing has to be passed between them.
scripts/settings_panel.gdThe settings screen, used by both the main menu and the pause menu.
scripts/ui_theme.gdThe look of every menu and button in the game, built in one place.
Build scripts

Shell scripts for the Android build, install and emulator.

FileWhat it does
build-android.shBuilds the Android APK. Run: ./build-android.sh
build-variants.shBuilds two APKs so a black screen can be narrowed down to the renderer. build/kedai-runtuh-vulkan.apk - Forward Mobile, best on a real phone build/kedai-runtuh-gles.apk - GL Compatibility, best odds in an emulator
install-android.shBuilds and installs onto a phone plugged in over USB. Run: ./install-android.sh
run-emulator.shStarts the Android emulator and installs the game. Run: ./run-emulator.sh
